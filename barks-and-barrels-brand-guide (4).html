Brand Guide · Vol. 01
AN ANNUAL SURF & FUNDRAISER TRIP · COSTA RICA

A Solo Female Traveler surf trip to Puerto Viejo, on Costa Rica's southern Caribbean coast. Booking a seat is what funds local dog rescue work.

01 · Destination

Puerto Viejo & Talamanca

This isn't a generic "Costa Rica beach trip." It's a specific stretch of the southern Caribbean coast, and that specificity is what makes the trip description worth reading.

The base

Puerto Viejo de Talamanca, with the group staying near Playa Cocles. Cocles is a forgiving, sandy beach break, good for beginners and intermediates alike, while Salsa Brava, the reef break right in town, is reserved for advanced surfers only. That split makes it realistic to put a mixed-level group of solo travelers in the water together.

Why here, not the Pacific side

Puerto Viejo has a different cultural identity than Costa Rica's more-traveled Pacific surf towns: Afro-Caribbean heritage, Caribbean food and music, cacao farms, rainforest, and the nearby Bribri Indigenous territory of Talamanca. One base, no half-the-trip-in-transit between regions.

What fills the days beyond surfing

  • Surf lessons through a local school based at Cocles or Puerto Viejo. Options in the area include Talamanca Republic Surf School & Tours, Nativos Surf School, and Surf the Jungle.
  • An overnight in Bribri territory, most likely Yorkín, reached by canoe upriver, organized directly through a community tourism association such as AGITUBRIT or the Indigenous Ecotourism Network of Talamanca, staying with a community-run host like Asociación STIBRAWPA or Aventuras Naturales Yorkín rather than a hotel.
  • Time built around cacao, medicinal-plant knowledge, traditional cooking, and craft work led by the community itself, not a scripted village tour.
  • Afro-Caribbean food and music culture specific to this coast, distinct from the rest of the country.
  • Rainforest on one side, reef and open Caribbean on the other, within a short walk or bike ride of the group's base in Puerto Viejo.
02 · Logo

The mark, as-is

One badge, three ideas at once: a dog standing watch, a wave big enough to surf, and a sun going down on a good day. The arched wordmark holds it together like a vintage surf-shop patch.

Before this goes to print anywhere

  • This file is a raster image (PNG), not a vector. It'll look great on a phone screen or a social post, but blown up for a banner or embroidered on a hat, the edges will get soft or blurry. Worth getting, or having me help rebuild, a vector version (.SVG or .AI) before it goes on anything printed large.
  • One background version only, so far. Right now it only exists on this peach-to-navy background. She'll likely also need a version that sits cleanly on plain white and on a solid dark navy, for places the sunset gradient won't fit (email signatures, small icons, single-color stamps).
  • No icon-only version yet. For favicons or anywhere too small for the wordmark to read, she'll want just the dog-and-wave mark on its own, without the arched text.

05 · Voice

How it talks

Leans into surf culture more than the softer "Waves for Wags" direction did, a little salty, a little cheeky, still warm underneath.

Say it like this

"12 spots. One beach house in Costa Rica. Book your seat, and part of it goes straight to dog rescue."

Say it like this

"Same trip you'd expect from us: surf, sun, a house full of women who get it. This one just happens to help dogs too."

How it actually works

  • This books and reads like any other Solo Female Traveler trip on the site, same format, same "Book Now" flow as Egypt, Turkey, Morocco.
  • The cause isn't a mechanic layered on top. No per-wave, per-mile, or per-activity pledges. Going on the trip is the fundraiser, a portion of what everyone pays to attend goes to dog rescue.
  • So the copy anywhere on the site or in the description should read like a trip listing first, with the giving-back part stated plainly once, not built into an activity metaphor that implies tracking or per-action donations.

Two language rules for the Bribri portion

  • Call it an overnight homestay or community stay in Bribri territory, arranged directly through a Bribri or community tourism organization. Don't describe it as "staying the night with a local tribe." It's not just softer phrasing, it reflects how the communities themselves describe this kind of tourism, and it's a better match for what's actually happening.
  • Frame anything involving medicinal plants as learning about traditional knowledge, never as diagnosis or treatment for a traveler's condition. Keeps the experience respectful and doesn't turn a living cultural practice into a wellness service.
